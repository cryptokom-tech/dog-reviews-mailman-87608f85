# Candidates and local judging

Criteria, in the judge's order: C1 a funny one-line dog review of the mailman at the centre; C2 the dog's side of the visit is the joke (grievance, bribe, suspicion, or small triumph); C3 a dog's voice and priorities, not a person in a fur suit; C4 one tight, complete line with a turn at the end; C5 the laugh comes from this dog and this mailman, not a generic stranger-at-the-door pun.

## Candidates

1. Brave enough to cross my yard daily, smart enough to flee my bark, rude enough to bring paper instead of chicken.
2. He has come 300 days in a row, never brought a treat, and still acts surprised I remember him. I do. By smell.
3. One star: he crosses my yard smelling of 30 other dogs, drops paper I cannot eat, and calls it a visit.
4. Daily he invades, leaves paper nobody can chew, and escapes my patrol. My humans keep thanking the intruder.
5. He opens my gate like he owns the yard, then jumps when the owner barks. The owner is me.
6. He puts bills in my house and runs from my bark, so either he's scared or the paper is poison. I bark louder now.
7. The truck stops, he feeds paper to my door, my tail says hello and my mouth says war. He only listens to the mouth.
8. No treat, no belly rub, just paper through the slot and a quick escape. Sir, this is a fence, not a suggestion.
9. He knows my name, my gate, my bark, and still brings zero biscuits. Bold strategy for a man in shorts.
10. He smells like every porch on the street and still expects trust at my gate. Nose says no. Bark agrees.
11. Five barks for effort: he survived my fence again, but brought another box that wasn't for me. Again.
12. I warned him at the fence 400 times; he left 400 times. At some point, respect the undefeated champion.
13. He slides paper through my door, ignores my warnings, then flees when I bark. Finally, a human who understands orders.
14. Every morning he rattles my gate, stuffs paper in my house, and leaves before I can sniff his shoes. Coward's route.
15. One wag for the truck, zero for the man: 200 visits, no biscuit, and he still walks like my bark is weather.
16. He comes at the same time, smells of strangers, touches my door, and leaves when challenged. Either mailman or very punctual burglar.

## Pairwise notes on the leaders

- 1 vs 2: both are grievances in a dog's voice, but 1 is more plainly a review of this mailman — daily yard, bark, paper — while 2 could fit any daily visitor. C1 and C5 decide it before 2's smell sting can.
- 1 vs 3: 3's star rating is explicit, but 1's triad is also an evaluation, so C1 is close; C4 then decides it, because "instead of chicken" lands harder than "calls it a visit," and 1 adds a bark triumph 3 lacks.
- 1 vs 4: 4's betrayed-patrol sting is funny, but "intruder" is generic; 1 keeps the laugh on the mailman's paper and the dog's bark. C5 decides.
- 1 vs 14: 14 is the closest on specificity, but its setup is longer and its sting is a label; 1 compresses complaint, triumph, and grievance into three beats and ends on the dog's top priority.

## Why the winner won

Candidate 1 runs a small story arc in one breath: complaint (he crosses my yard daily), turn (he flees my bark — the dog wins), sting in the last words (paper instead of chicken). Every beat is a dog priority — territory, bark, food — and every beat belongs to this mailman: the daily route, the paper he brings. The parallel "enough to" rhythm makes it read as a review without spending characters on a rating label, and the final word is the laugh.
